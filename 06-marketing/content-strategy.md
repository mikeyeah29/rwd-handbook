# RWD Content Strategy

## Purpose

This document defines how RWD will use useful, experience-led content to attract suitable businesses and agency partners, build trust and move readers towards the most relevant service.

It is the canonical source for editorial strategy. The practical website specification for the Insights section is documented in [Insights Page](../09-website/pages/insights.md). Audience research, service definitions and brand guidance remain authoritative for their respective subjects and should be referenced rather than duplicated here.

## Commercial Objectives

Content should support RWD's commercial priorities in this order:

1. Generate suitable enquiries for Custom WordPress Websites, Website Momentum and White-label WordPress Development.
2. Demonstrate Mike's judgement, technical ability and way of working before a prospect makes contact.
3. Build organic search visibility around the problems and decisions that lead people to RWD's services.
4. Create useful source material for social posts, email and future video content.
5. Build a durable library of intellectual property that makes the RWD website more useful over time.

Traffic is not the primary goal in isolation. The aim is to attract people who could realistically become a client, referral partner or useful long-term audience member.

## Audience

The content programme serves three main groups.

### Businesses Planning a Website

Businesses that need a new website or substantial rebuild and are trying to understand scope, cost, platform choices, process and how to choose the right partner.

The primary route from this content is [Custom WordPress Websites](../03-services/custom-websites.md).

### Owners of Existing Websites

Businesses that know their website could be more useful but are unsure whether the problem is technical health, performance, visibility, content, conversion or the way the website supports day-to-day work.

The primary route from this content is [Website Momentum](../03-services/website-momentum.md). Managed Hosting and Maintenance may be the more proportionate route when a suitable RWD-built website only needs technical aftercare.

### Creative and Marketing Agencies

Agencies that need dependable WordPress capacity, careful implementation of supplied designs or help with custom functionality and existing client websites.

The primary route from this content is White-label WordPress Development. Detailed needs, concerns and language are documented in [Audience Research: White-label WordPress Agencies](../04-audiences/agencies.md).

Priority industry audiences, beginning with therapists and later including estate agents and small businesses, should receive audience-specific content within this system. Their content should reflect the relevant audience research rather than applying generic small-business advice with a different title.

## Editorial Position

The central editorial idea is:

> A website should become a more useful business asset over time.

RWD content should help readers make better decisions about planning, building and improving WordPress websites. It should connect technical choices to business and human outcomes rather than treating technology as the end product.

The content should feel like Mike explaining what matters, what does not and what he would do next. It should be calm, candid, specific and useful.

## Editorial Principles

### Begin With a Real Question

Every article should answer a question, problem or decision that a potential client genuinely faces. Search demand can help prioritise subjects, but it should not be the sole reason for creating them.

### Demonstrate First-hand Judgement

Use project experience, audits, screenshots, examples, decision frameworks and clearly explained trade-offs. Do not merely summarise advice already available elsewhere.

### Connect Technical Work to Outcomes

Explain why performance, accessibility, content structure, maintainability, analytics or custom development matter to the business and its users.

### Give a Useful Answer

Do not withhold the conclusion to force an enquiry. A reader should leave with a clearer understanding of the problem and a sensible next step, even when they do not hire RWD.

### Recommend Proportionate Solutions

Do not imply that every problem requires a rebuild, custom development or Website Momentum. Say when a smaller improvement, a supported plugin, straightforward maintenance or no immediate action is more appropriate.

### Use Evidence Carefully

Do not invent performance gains, rankings, enquiry increases, customer quotes or business results. Use verified examples and describe the work completed when a commercial outcome cannot be substantiated.

### Write for People Before Search Engines

Use the language people use to describe their problem, organise the page clearly and answer the subject completely. There is no target word count. The appropriate length is the shortest length that answers the question properly and provides useful evidence.

## Content Types

The programme should contain a deliberate mix of the following types.

### Decision Articles

Help a reader compare options and make a purchase or planning decision.

Examples:

- Does your website need a redesign or focused improvement?
- WordPress versus Wix or Squarespace for a service business
- How to choose a WordPress developer
- Custom development versus an off-the-shelf solution

These articles often have strong commercial intent and should link clearly to the relevant service.

### Diagnostic Articles

Help a reader understand why a website is not producing the expected result.

Examples:

- Why a website receives visitors but few enquiries
- How to distinguish a traffic problem from a conversion problem
- What to check first on a slow WordPress website
- Why a website has become difficult to edit

These articles naturally support Website Momentum when they explain how competing priorities should be assessed and ordered.

### Practical Guides

Help a reader complete or prepare for a meaningful task without turning RWD into a general DIY tutorial site.

Examples:

- What to include in a website brief
- How to prepare content for a website project
- What an agency should include in a Figma handover
- What every therapist website should explain

### Audits and Teardowns

Use real or representative pages to show what works, what creates friction and what should be improved first.

Obtain permission before publishing an identifiable critique unless the example is RWD's own work. Keep the tone constructive and avoid embarrassing the organisation or individual being discussed.

### Experience and Point-of-view Articles

Explain the reasoning behind RWD's approach.

Examples:

- Why RWD uses Gutenberg for new builds
- When a plugin is the right answer and when custom code adds value
- Why maintenance alone does not make a website more effective
- What makes a WordPress build easier for another developer to inherit

These should contain useful reasoning and examples rather than existing only to state an opinion.

### Project Lessons

Explore one useful decision, constraint or technical problem from a completed project. The full project story remains under Work; the article should teach a transferable lesson and link to the relevant case study.

## Funnel and Internal Linking

Content should support the whole decision journey.

| Stage | Reader need | Useful content | Primary next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem recognition | Understand what is wrong or missing | Diagnostic articles, audits and industry observations | Read a related guide or assess the current website |
| Option research | Compare approaches and likely investment | Decision articles and practical guides | Explore the relevant service |
| Partner evaluation | Decide whether RWD is a suitable partner | Project lessons, process articles and technical point of view | View relevant work or make an enquiry |
| Ongoing improvement | Decide what should happen after launch | Website Momentum, measurement and prioritisation content | Explore Website Momentum |

Every article should include:

- A contextual link to the most relevant service or audience page
- Links to closely related articles
- A link to relevant work when a project provides evidence
- A useful next step appropriate to the reader's stage

Do not force every article towards the same generic call to action.

## Article Brief

Before drafting an article, record:

- The reader and situation
- The question or problem being answered
- The likely search intent
- The primary category
- The relevant service or audience page
- The desired next step
- First-hand evidence, examples or screenshots available
- Claims that require verification
- Related articles and work to link to
- Social formats the article can support

An article should not be commissioned solely because a keyword tool reports volume. It must also fit RWD's audience, expertise and commercial direction.

## Repurposing for Social and Email

The article is the source asset. Social posts should extract useful ideas from it rather than merely announce that a new article exists.

One article can produce:

- One LinkedIn post explaining the main argument
- One carousel or document post presenting a framework or checklist
- One short video explaining the strongest observation
- Three concise posts built from individual points
- One email or newsletter section
- One discussion question or poll
- A future long-form video where the subject benefits from demonstration

Adapt the idea to the channel. A social post should provide value without requiring the reader to click, while the full article provides the complete explanation, evidence and route into the website.

Agency subjects will often suit LinkedIn. Visual audits, page patterns and annotated examples can become short-form video or carousel content. Audience-specific guidance should be shared in the places where that audience already gathers rather than distributed identically across every channel.

## Publishing Rhythm

Begin with two substantial articles per month:

1. One broader search-led decision or diagnostic article.
2. One audience-specific, agency or experience-led article.

This creates a sustainable baseline of 24 articles per year and enough source material for regular social and email distribution.

Quality, first-hand evidence and useful internal linking take priority over an arbitrary publishing frequency. Existing articles should be improved when new evidence, better examples or changed guidance materially affects the answer. Do not change dates simply to make an article appear fresh.

## Measurement

Measure content against commercial and audience outcomes rather than page views alone.

### Primary Measures

- Suitable enquiries influenced by content
- Enquiries attributed to a specific article or topic cluster
- Visits from articles to service, audience and work pages
- Website Momentum Scorecard completions or email sign-ups
- Assisted conversions where content supported a later enquiry
- Revenue or qualified pipeline influenced by content

### Supporting Measures

- Organic impressions and clicks for relevant searches
- Search terms that reveal useful new questions
- Engagement with calls to action and related content
- Returning readers
- Social saves, replies and meaningful conversations
- Articles that earn relevant links or referrals

High traffic with no commercial or audience relevance is not success. Low-volume content can be valuable when it consistently reaches a narrow, high-value audience.

## Governance

- Mike is the named author unless another contributor has genuinely written or materially contributed to the article.
- Each article should show an accurate publication date and a last-updated date only when the content has been meaningfully revised.
- Review priority articles at least annually and when the underlying technology, regulation, pricing or RWD offer changes.
- Preserve URL stability when updating content.
- Merge or redirect substantially overlapping articles rather than allowing them to compete with each other.
- Keep research notes and unverified claims out of published copy until they have been checked.

## Content to Avoid

Avoid building the programme around:

- Generic WordPress installation tutorials
- Broad lists of free plugins with no strategic relevance
- Weekly WordPress news without a clear client implication
- Long coding tutorials aimed primarily at developers
- Unrelated AI tool roundups
- Articles created only to target a keyword variation
- Thin industry pages that repeat generic advice
- Unsupported promises about rankings, traffic, enquiries or revenue
- Content that teaches the reader everything except why the decision matters

Technical content is appropriate when it helps a business or agency make a better decision. For example, prefer **When Does a Business Need a Custom Gutenberg Block?** over **How to Code a Gutenberg Block**.
