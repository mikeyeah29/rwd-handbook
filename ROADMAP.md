# Rockett Web Design — MVP Roadmap

This is the minimum roadmap for the first version of the new RWD website and positioning.

It is deliberately small. The goal is to publish a clear, useful website quickly, learn from real conversations and improve it later. It is not a plan to finish the entire business, every audience page or every possible piece of content.

The plan assumes approximately **5 hours per week** and should take **8 weeks, or about 40 hours**, to complete. If a task runs over, simplify it or move it to the next phase. Do not add new scope to compensate.

## The MVP

The first version is complete when these paths are clear:

1. A visitor arrives at the homepage and understands what RWD does.
2. They can recognise whether RWD is relevant to them.
3. They can choose the most relevant service.
4. They can see enough work and proof to trust the direction.
5. They can understand how RWD works.
6. They can take one obvious next step.

### Pages and features included

- **Home** — the doorway into the new positioning.
- **My Approach** — the current technical and working philosophy moved off the homepage.
- **White-Label Development** — service page.
- **Custom WordPress Websites** — service page.
- **Website Momentum** — service page.
- **Websites for Therapists** — updated audience page and reusable template for future industries.
- **Work** — existing index reviewed and connected to the new positioning.
- **Work single page template** — existing template retained and improved only where needed.
- **Existing work items** — a small, credible selection is enough for launch.
- **A working CTA/enquiry route** — this can be an existing contact form, email link or booking route; a separate Contact page is not required for the MVP.

### Explicitly not required for the MVP

- Estate agent, agency or small-business audience pages.
- Five fully written portfolio case studies.
- Blog posts or the Insights section.
- A services hub page, if the homepage can link directly to the three service pages.
- A client portal, Website Momentum management tool or advanced automation.
- A complex pricing system or proposal generator.
- Perfect copy, exhaustive SEO or a fully refined visual system.

## Week 1 — Fix the MVP and outline the pages

**Priority: essential**

Spend this week deciding what will be said, not designing every detail.

### Tasks

- Confirm the one-sentence positioning for RWD.
- Define the three main services in one or two sentences each:
  - White-Label Development.
  - Custom WordPress Websites.
  - Website Momentum.
- Define the primary CTA, such as **Let’s Talk**, and decide exactly where it goes.
- Sketch the homepage sections:
  - What RWD does.
  - Who it is for.
  - The three services.
  - Selected work.
  - Short approach/value statement.
  - CTA.
- Create a simple outline for My Approach, each service page and the therapist page.
- Choose the existing work items to show on the homepage and therapist page.
- Decide what the price calculator needs to estimate. Keep it to a small number of useful inputs and a sensible indicative range.

### Done when

- The page list is fixed.
- Each page has a purpose and a rough section list.
- The next action is obvious without needing more strategic thinking.

## Week 2 — Rework the homepage

**Priority: essential**

The homepage should act as a useful signpost, not try to explain the whole business.

### Tasks

- Replace the current homepage structure so it reflects the new positioning.
- State clearly what RWD does and the belief that a website should become a stronger business asset.
- Identify the main audiences at a high level without creating a separate page for each one yet.
- Add three service sections with concise descriptions and direct links to the service pages.
- Add a small selection of existing work items.
- Add a short explanation of the approach and link to My Approach.
- Add one clear CTA near the middle and at the end of the page.

### Keep it lean

- Do not add every service detail to the homepage.
- Do not wait for more portfolio items.
- Do not redesign sections that already work unless they conflict with the new message.

### Done when

Someone unfamiliar with RWD can answer these questions from the homepage:

- What does RWD do?
- Is it relevant to me?
- What should I look at next?

## Week 3 — Build My Approach

**Priority: essential**

Move the detailed philosophy and technical explanation out of the homepage and give it one focused home.

### Tasks

- Use the existing outline as the starting point.
- Explain the principles in plain language:
  - Gutenberg-first.
  - Lightweight and maintainable.
  - Performance-focused.
  - Accessible and practical.
  - Built to evolve.
- Explain how the working process feels for a client or agency.
- Include the relevant capabilities: custom themes, blocks, plugins, performance, technical SEO and integrations.
- Keep the focus on client outcomes rather than a list of technologies.
- Link to the most relevant service pages and to Work.

### Done when

My Approach explains why RWD works this way and makes the technical difference understandable without becoming a technical manual.

## Weeks 4–5 — Build the three service pages

**Priority: essential**

Use one repeatable structure so these pages are fast to write and easy to compare.

### Shared page structure

Each service page needs only:

1. Who it is for.
2. The problem it solves.
3. What RWD does.
4. What the client can expect.
5. Relevant proof or work.
6. A clear next step.

Avoid creating long feature lists or trying to answer every possible objection in version one.

### Week 4 — White-Label Development

Focus on:

- Dependable WordPress development for agencies.
- Turning supplied designs into careful, maintainable builds.
- Extra delivery capacity without hiring.
- Clear communication and reliable handover.
- Working behind the agency's brand where required.

Link to My Approach and the most relevant existing work.

### Week 5 — Custom Websites and Website Momentum

Build both pages using the same simple framework.

**Custom WordPress Websites** should explain bespoke websites for businesses that need a clear, manageable and future-proof foundation.

**Website Momentum** should explain ongoing care and improvement: keeping a website secure and healthy, then making focused improvements to performance, visibility, conversion and usefulness over time.

Make the relationship between the two clear: a custom website can become a Website Momentum client after launch, but do not turn the page into a full subscription product specification yet.

### Done when

- The homepage links to all three pages.
- Each service has a distinct audience and purpose.
- Each page ends with the same obvious next step.

## Week 6 — Update the therapist page and make it reusable

**Priority: essential**

Treat the therapist page as the first finished example of the industry-page pattern. It does not need to become a complete system before it is useful.

### Tasks

- Update the page to match the new positioning and Custom WordPress Websites offer.
- Add a small number of relevant testimonials, using only approved wording.
- Add relevant existing work items.
- Add a simple price calculator or estimator.
- Keep the calculator intentionally lightweight: a few inputs, an indicative range and a prompt to discuss the project. It should not attempt to produce a final quote.
- Add a clear CTA and link back to Custom WordPress Websites and My Approach.
- Note the reusable sections and fields that will later support estate agent, agency and small-business pages.

### Do not do yet

- Do not build the other industry pages.
- Do not spend the week building a sophisticated calculator backend.
- Do not delay the page because every testimonial or portfolio item is not ready.

### Done when

- A therapist can understand the offer, see credible proof, get an indicative price and take the next step.
- The page structure can be reused with different audience-specific content later.

## Week 7 — Connect the site and improve conversion

**Priority: essential**

This is a joining-up week, not a new-content week.

### Tasks

- Review the Work index and make sure the selected work items support the new positioning.
- Check the existing single work template for a clear summary, role, outcome and CTA. Only improve missing essentials.
- Connect homepage sections to the relevant service pages, My Approach, therapist page and Work.
- Make sure every important page has one primary next step.
- Check the navigation remains small and understandable.
- Test the enquiry route from mobile and desktop.
- Remove duplicated copy and anything that makes the offer unclear.

### Done when

It is possible to move through the site as a visitor without dead ends, confusing choices or unexplained jumps between offers.

## Week 8 — Minimum launch check and publish

**Priority: essential**

Launch after a short quality check. Do not turn this into another redesign phase.

### Essential checks

- All links, buttons and forms work.
- The price calculator behaves sensibly and explains that results are indicative.
- Pages work on mobile.
- Headings, page titles and URLs are sensible.
- Text is readable and colour contrast is acceptable.
- Images have useful alt text.
- The site is not carrying obviously oversized images or unnecessary assets.
- Privacy/cookie requirements and analytics are handled using the existing setup.
- The site has a basic sitemap and can be found by search engines when ready.

### Launch decision

Publish if the site is clear, usable and credible. Record anything unfinished in a later list; do not keep the site in draft because it could be polished further.

## Later reminders — not part of the MVP

Keep these as a backlog, not as launch blockers:

- Reuse the therapist page for estate agents, agencies and small businesses.
- Add more work items and write deeper case studies when there is a clear reason to do so.
- Improve the Work template with stronger outcomes, testimonials and related services.
- Develop Website Momentum into a more detailed package, workflow and client reporting system.
- Add the Insights/blog section and publish posts from real client questions and project experience.
- Add lead magnets, email nurture and more advanced conversion tracking.
- Review SEO based on actual search demand and enquiries.

## Rules for avoiding overthinking

- If a page answers its core question, publish it.
- If a feature does not help someone understand, trust or contact RWD, defer it.
- Use the content and components already available before creating new systems.
- Prefer a simple working calculator over a perfect calculator.
- Prefer three useful work items over waiting for a complete portfolio.
- Make one meaningful improvement per week; do not redesign the whole site repeatedly.
- When the MVP is live, let real conversations decide what deserves the next hour.
