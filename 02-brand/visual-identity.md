# Visual Identity

RWD's visual identity should feel like **calm mission control**, not a spaceship.

The brand can use the energy of the Rockett name, but it should not lean into obvious space imagery. The visual identity should communicate progress, precision and business momentum without becoming gimmicky or overly futuristic.

## Core Visual Idea

**Progress is the brand.**

The rocket is a useful symbol, but it is not the whole identity. The stronger idea is forward movement: websites becoming useful assets that create value after launch.

## Visual Metaphors

### The Grid

Represents structure, engineering, systems and strong foundations.

The grid should suggest the invisible framework behind a successful website: planning, precision, technical craft and maintainable systems.

Use grids subtly in layouts, backgrounds, interfaces and supporting graphics.

### The Circle

Represents growth, monitoring and momentum.

Radiating circles, pulses and scanning patterns can suggest analysis, performance monitoring, AI, signals and compounding progress.

Use circles calmly and deliberately. They should feel active, not decorative.

### The Rocket

Represents progress, momentum and direction.

Use the rocket as a restrained symbol of movement and ambition. Avoid literal space themes such as planets, galaxies, astronauts or sci-fi effects.

## Design Language

RWD's design language should feel:

- Intelligent
- Modern
- Precise
- Calm
- Engineered
- Useful
- Approachable

The experience should feel thoughtfully built rather than heavily decorated.

## Visual Principles

- Clean layouts with generous whitespace.
- Thin lines and subtle grids for structure.
- Strong contrast and restrained colour.
- Modern typography with excellent readability.
- Gentle motion that suggests ongoing activity.
- Thoughtful interactions that reward exploration.
- Visual elements that serve a purpose.

Nothing should exist only because it looks impressive. Every graphic, animation and interaction should support the idea that a website can create momentum for the business behind it.

## Colour Scheme

The colour palette should feel technical, calm and premium without becoming cold or overly corporate.

RWD should avoid obvious space-brand colours such as deep galaxy blues, neon purples and sci-fi gradients. The palette should feel grounded and professional, with enough energy to support the idea of momentum.

### Core Palette

- **Near Black** `#111315`  
  Primary text, dark backgrounds and high-contrast interface elements.

- **Graphite** `#2A2F35`  
  Secondary dark tone for panels, borders, diagrams and supporting UI.

- **Soft White** `#F7F5F0`  
  Main light background. Warmer than pure white, but still clean and modern.

- **Mist Grey** `#D9DEE3`  
  Borders, grid lines, dividers, muted backgrounds and technical detailing.

- **Signal Green** `#39D98A`  
  Primary accent for progress, active states, positive movement and key highlights.

- **Momentum Amber** `#F5B84B`  
  Secondary accent for warmth, emphasis and selected moments of energy.

### Usage

- Use **Near Black** and **Soft White** as the main contrast pair.
- Use **Mist Grey** for structure, grids and subtle technical detail.
- Use **Signal Green** sparingly to show progress, activity and forward movement.
- Use **Momentum Amber** even more sparingly for warmth or emphasis.
- Avoid large areas of bright colour. The brand should feel precise, not loud.
- Avoid decorative gradients unless they are extremely subtle and serve a clear purpose.

## Typography

Typography should feel modern, precise and highly readable.

The type system should support the feeling of calm technical authority: clear enough for business owners, refined enough for a premium digital service, and structured enough to feel engineered.

### Direction

- Use a clean modern sans-serif as the primary typeface.
- Prefer open letterforms, strong readability and a neutral-but-human feel.
- Avoid fonts that feel too playful, futuristic, decorative or startup-generic.
- Use weight, spacing and hierarchy rather than excessive colour or decoration.
- Keep headings confident but not oversized unless used in a true hero section.

### Recommended Pairing

- **Primary Sans:** `Inter`, `Geist`, `Satoshi` or `Avenir Next`
- **Technical Accent:** `JetBrains Mono`, `IBM Plex Mono` or `Geist Mono`

The primary sans should handle most brand communication, website copy and interface text. The mono accent can be used for small technical details, labels, metrics, code-like references or system-style UI elements.

### Typographic Feel

- Headings should be clear, composed and direct.
- Body copy should feel easy to read, with generous line height.
- Labels and metadata can be slightly more technical.
- Avoid tight letter spacing, condensed display fonts or overly dramatic type.
- Do not let the typography become more expressive than the message.

## Iconography

Icons should feel engineered, precise and intentional.

Use a bespoke geometric style rather than generic interface icons where brand expression matters. Icons should feel like part of a single system, not a collection of unrelated graphics.

### Icon Style

- Thin, consistent stroke widths
- Simple geometric shapes
- Straight lines, circles, arcs and nodes
- Subtle grid alignment
- Minimal visual noise
- Abstract metaphors over obvious objects

### Concept Direction

Prefer abstract visual ideas:

- Protection as a protected orbit, not just a shield
- Performance as an expanding pulse, not just a speedometer
- Automation as connected nodes, not just a cog
- Monitoring as a radar sweep, not just an eye
- Growth as a growing signal, not just a graph

## Motion

Motion should feel calm, deliberate and purposeful.

Useful motion ideas include:

- Lines drawing into place
- Gentle radar sweeps
- Expanding pulses
- Nodes connecting
- Data flowing through paths
- Subtle indicators of ongoing activity

Avoid motion that feels distracting, flashy or purely decorative.

## Visual Test

Before introducing a design element, ask:

- Does this communicate progress?
- Does this reinforce momentum or asset value?
- Does this make the interface feel alive without becoming distracting?
- Does this support clarity?
- Does this feel engineered rather than decorative?
- Would this still make sense if the business was not called Rockett?

If the answer is no to most of these, it probably does not belong.
