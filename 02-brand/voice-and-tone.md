# Voice And Tone

RWD communicates with **calm authority**.

The voice should be confident without being arrogant, knowledgeable without being overwhelming, and personal without feeling too casual. It should sound like an experienced engineer explaining important ideas in plain English.

The goal is not to sound louder than other agencies. The goal is to sound more trustworthy.

## Voice Qualities

### Personal

Write like a real person. Use "I" where it feels natural, especially in proposals, emails and founder-led content.

### Confident

Be clear about what RWD does well. Avoid overselling, exaggerated claims or promises that cannot be proven.

### Clear

Use plain English. If technical language is necessary, explain it simply and connect it to the benefit for the client.

### Forward-Looking

Keep reinforcing the idea that a website should create value after launch. Focus on momentum, asset value and long-term usefulness rather than one-off delivery.

### Supportive

The tone should feel collaborative and reassuring. RWD is a partner, not just a supplier.

## Writing Principles

- Write like you are speaking to an intelligent business owner.
- Use short, clear sentences.
- Explain benefits before features.
- Be specific rather than exaggerated.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon.
- Lead with belief, then support it with expertise.
- Make every sentence build trust.

## Preferred Style

Use language that feels calm, practical and useful:

- "A great website creates momentum."
- "We identify the most valuable improvements, make them, measure them and repeat."
- "The technical work happens in the background. You see the progress."
- "Your website should become a stronger business asset over time."
- "Maintenance keeps a website running. Momentum helps it grow."
- "Don't build another website. Build an asset."

## Avoid

Avoid language that feels generic, inflated or fear-based:

- "Revolutionary"
- "Game-changing"
- "Industry-leading"
- "Next-level"
- "Dominate your market"
- "Your website is costing you thousands"
- "Don't get left behind"

## Voice Test

Before publishing copy, ask:

- Does this sound like a real conversation?
- Does it communicate confidence without exaggeration?
- Would an experienced professional naturally say this?
- Does it reinforce momentum, usefulness or asset value?
- Does it make the reader feel informed rather than sold to?

If the answer is no to any of these, rewrite it.

## Example

Instead of:

> We create cutting-edge, industry-leading WordPress solutions that maximise your online potential.

Write:

> I build WordPress websites designed to become stronger business assets over time.
