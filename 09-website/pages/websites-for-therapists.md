# Websites For Therapists

## Page Strategy

- **Purpose:** Present RWD as a thoughtful WordPress website partner for therapists in private practice, demonstrate relevant experience and turn suitable visitors into project conversations.
- **Audience:** UK counsellors, psychotherapists, therapists and practitioner psychologists who are starting a private practice, replacing a DIY or dated website, or trying to make an existing site more useful. Coaches are not part of this page's core audience.
- **Funnel stage:** Problem-aware to solution-aware. The visitor knows they need a website or that the current one is underperforming, but may not yet know whether they need a freelancer, template, platform or ongoing partner.
- **Traffic source:** Organic searches such as “websites for therapists,” “web design for therapists,” “therapist website design UK,” “counsellor website design” and “psychologist website design”; referrals; portfolio links; internal links from Custom WordPress Websites, Work, My Approach and relevant articles.
- **Primary offer:** A custom WordPress website designed and built around the practice, its ideal clients and its long-term goals.
- **Primary CTA:** Tell me about your practice.
- **Secondary CTA:** Get an indicative price.
- **Visitor problem:** The practitioner needs a website that represents them professionally, helps the right people find and understand their work, and makes contacting them feel straightforward. They may feel overwhelmed by DIY tools, unclear about content and SEO, or disappointed by a generic site that produces few suitable enquiries.
- **Desired outcome:** A professional, personal and easy-to-manage website that supports referrals and search visibility, communicates fit clearly, creates suitable enquiries and can evolve with the practice.
- **Key objections:** “I can build it myself”; “most clients come from directories or referrals”; “I do not have the copy ready”; “SEO services have disappointed me before”; “a custom website will be too expensive”; “I am not technical”; “I do not want to look salesy”; “will I own it?”; “can I update it?”; “what happens after launch?”

The page should move the visitor through five beliefs:

1. “Mike understands why a therapy website is different from a generic business site.”
2. “He understands the problem I am currently experiencing.”
3. “The proposed website would represent me and make things clearer for prospective clients.”

### Recognition

## Your practice has moved forward. Has your website?

Perhaps you are preparing to open your private practice and do not know where to begin. Perhaps you put together a website when you started, but it no longer reflects your experience or the work you most want to do.

Or perhaps the site looks fine, yet the right people are not finding it—or they visit without making contact.

These are different problems, but they create the same feeling: your website matters, and it has become another complicated job sitting beside the work you actually trained to do.

This service is designed to take that job off your shoulders while keeping your voice, judgement and professional identity at the centre of the project.

**Possible recognition list:**

- You rely on directories or referrals and need a professional home for your practice
- Your current site feels dated, generic or difficult to update
- You are unsure how to explain your specialisms without excluding everyone else
- You have tried keywords, blogs or an SEO plugin but cannot see meaningful progress
- Your website gets visits but too few suitable enquiries
- You want a site you feel comfortable sharing with clients and professional referrers

### Core outcome

## Make it easier for the right person to choose their next step

Someone visiting a therapy website may be anxious, uncertain or reaching out for support for the first time. They should not have to search for the basic information that helps them make an informed decision.

A useful website helps them understand:

- Who you work with
- What you may be able to help with
- How you approach the work
- Your qualifications and professional registrations
- Whether sessions are online, in person or both
- Where you are based
- What sessions cost
- What will happen when they contact you

The aim is not to persuade everybody. It is to help suitable clients recognise a possible fit and give them a clear, comfortable way to enquire.

### What a therapy website needs

## Professional enough to build trust. Personal enough to feel human.

Therapy is a personal service, so a polished design is only part of the answer. The words, structure, imagery and small practical details all need to feel consistent with the person a client will eventually meet.

### Be found for the work you want to do

The site will be structured around the real services, specialisms and locations relevant to your practice. Strong technical and on-page SEO foundations give search engines clearer information without stuffing pages with awkward keywords.

### Help people understand whether you may be a good fit

Clear, human content should speak to the prospective client's situation as well as explaining your modalities and credentials. Specificity helps the right person recognise themselves in your work.

### Answer the practical questions

Fees, availability, session format, location, accessibility and what happens next should be easy to find. Clarity reduces uncertainty and avoids repetitive enquiries.

### Offer a simple next step

The enquiry route will match the way you work, whether that is an initial email, a short form, a telephone conversation or consultation booking. It should collect only the information you genuinely need at that stage.

The result is not a finished brochure that slowly falls behind. It is a strong foundation that can evolve as your practice, services and audience develop.

### FAQs

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you only build websites for therapists?

No. I build and support WordPress websites for several types of business. This service is tailored to the particular needs of counsellors, psychotherapists and psychologists in private practice, and is supported by relevant project experience.

### Can you help if I do not know what to put on the website?

Yes. The project begins by deciding who the site is for, what they need to understand and what pages will communicate that clearly. The final service description must specify whether this includes content planning, copy editing or complete copywriting.

### Will the website get me to the top of Google?

No responsible developer can guarantee a particular ranking. The site will be built with strong technical and on-page foundations, clear service structure and relevant local information. Search visibility can then be monitored and improved over time.

### Do I still need a website if I use a therapist directory?

A directory can remain a useful source of discovery. Your own website gives prospective clients and professional referrers a fuller, more personal picture of your work, and gives your practice an online home you control.

### Can you redesign my existing therapist website?

Yes. I will first identify useful content, links and search visibility worth preserving. The new site can be prepared away from the live website before a carefully planned launch.

### Will I be able to make changes myself?

Yes. The site is built in WordPress with a practical editing experience for routine content changes. A handover will show you how to use it. More substantial improvements can be handled through ad hoc work or Website Momentum.

### Can you connect my booking system?

In most cases, yes. The right route depends on whether you want people to book an introductory call directly, submit an enquiry first or use an existing practice-management platform. We will choose a journey that matches how you assess fit and manage availability.

### What about privacy and sensitive enquiries?

The site can use data-minimising forms, appropriate security measures and carefully selected services. You will still need suitable privacy information and a lawful process for handling personal data. RWD does not provide legal or clinical-compliance advice.

### Will I own the website?

The proposal should state ownership, licensing, hosting and third-party costs plainly. The intended position is that the client owns their domain, content and completed website rather than being trapped in a proprietary subscription. Confirm the exact contractual wording before publishing this answer as a commitment.

### What happens after launch?

You can manage the site after handover or choose ongoing Website Momentum support. Ongoing work can include maintenance, monitoring, SEO, content, performance and conversion improvements, prioritised according to what will be most useful.
