# Websites For Therapists

## Page Strategy

- **Purpose:** Present RWD as a thoughtful WordPress website partner for therapists in private practice, demonstrate relevant experience and turn suitable visitors into project conversations.
- **Audience:** UK counsellors, psychotherapists, therapists and practitioner psychologists who are starting a private practice, replacing a DIY or dated website, or trying to make an existing site more useful. Coaches are not part of this page's core audience.
- **Funnel stage:** Problem-aware to solution-aware. The visitor knows they need a website or that the current one is underperforming, but may not yet know whether they need a freelancer, template, platform or ongoing partner.
- **Traffic source:** Organic searches such as “websites for therapists,” “web design for therapists,” “therapist website design UK,” “counsellor website design” and “psychologist website design”; referrals; portfolio links; internal links from Custom WordPress Websites, Work, My Approach and relevant articles.
- **Primary offer:** A custom WordPress website designed and built around the practice, its ideal clients and its long-term goals.
- **Primary CTA:** Tell me about your practice.
- **Secondary CTA:** Get an indicative price.
- **Visitor problem:** The practitioner needs a website that represents them professionally, helps the right people find and understand their work, and makes contacting them feel straightforward. They may feel overwhelmed by DIY tools, unclear about content and SEO, or disappointed by a generic site that produces few suitable enquiries.
- **Desired outcome:** A professional, personal and easy-to-manage website that supports referrals and search visibility, communicates fit clearly, creates suitable enquiries and can evolve with the practice.
- **Key objections:** “I can build it myself”; “most clients come from directories or referrals”; “I do not have the copy ready”; “SEO services have disappointed me before”; “a custom website will be too expensive”; “I am not technical”; “I do not want to look salesy”; “will I own it?”; “can I update it?”; “what happens after launch?”

## Positioning

### Core proposition

> Thoughtful WordPress websites that help the right clients find, understand and contact your practice.

### Audience terminology

“Therapist” is the primary SEO term and page shorthand. Near the top of the page, explicitly name:

- Counsellors
- Psychotherapists
- Therapists
- Practitioner psychologists

Do not imply that these professional titles are identical. Reproduce protected titles, credentials and registrations exactly as supplied and verified by the client.

Do not include coaches in the hero or primary SEO proposition. A psychologist who also coaches may appear as relevant work if the therapy or psychology side of the project is clear. Use coaching-only work on a future coaches page.

### Message hierarchy

1. The right clients can find and recognise themselves in the practice.
2. The website feels personal and accurately reflects how the practitioner works.
3. Important questions about fit, format, fees and next steps are answered clearly.
4. The design and technical work are handled through a calm, guided process.
5. The practitioner owns an editable WordPress website that can evolve after launch.

### Tone

- Calm, clear and human
- Professional without becoming clinical or corporate
- Reassuring without using fear
- Specific without promising rankings, enquiries or clinical outcomes
- Respectful of the visitor's expertise and ethical responsibilities

## SEO Plan

- **Proposed URL:** `/custom-wordpress-websites/websites-for-therapists/`
- **Primary search theme:** websites for therapists / web design for therapists
- **Secondary themes:** therapist website design UK; websites for counsellors; counsellor web design; psychotherapist website design; psychologist website design; private practice website design
- **Search intent:** Commercial investigation. The searcher is comparing approaches, providers, examples and costs.
- **Proposed SEO title:** Web Design for Therapists, Counsellors & Psychologists | RWD
- **Proposed meta description:** Custom WordPress websites for UK therapists, counsellors, psychotherapists and psychologists—built to communicate trust, support search visibility and make enquiries easier.
- **Proposed H1:** Websites for therapists that feel true to your practice

Use the profession variants naturally in headings, proof, FAQs and body copy. Do not create several near-duplicate pages simply to target each title. Separate pages should only be created later if RWD has distinct proof, content and offers for those audiences.

## Conversion Strategy

The page should move the visitor through five beliefs:

1. “Mike understands why a therapy website is different from a generic business site.”
2. “He understands the problem I am currently experiencing.”
3. “The proposed website would represent me and make things clearer for prospective clients.”
4. “The process feels manageable and the technical details will be handled responsibly.”
5. “I know what to do next and can explore the likely cost without committing.”

The page should not treat maximum enquiry volume as the goal. The more credible promise is better clarity, stronger visibility foundations and a simpler route for suitable clients to make contact.

## Page Outline

1. Hero: primary promise, named professions and two calls to action
2. Recognition: the situations that bring a practitioner to the page
3. Core outcome: help the right clients find, understand and contact the practice
4. What a therapy website needs: fit, trust, practical clarity and a gentle next step
5. Tailored solution: strategy, content structure, design, WordPress build and SEO foundations
6. Relevant work: approved therapist projects with context rather than unsupported result claims
7. About Mike: relevant experience and what working together feels like
8. What's included: concrete deliverables and boundaries
9. Process: a calm, staged route from discovery to launch
10. Indicative price estimator: lightweight scope inputs and an honest non-binding range
11. Ownership and ongoing support: editing, handover, Managed Hosting and Maintenance, and Website Momentum
12. FAQs: SEO, copy, existing sites, privacy, editing, ownership and ongoing costs
13. Final call to action: invite a low-pressure conversation about the practice
