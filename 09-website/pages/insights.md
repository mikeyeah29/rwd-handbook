# Insights Page

## Page Strategy

- **Purpose:** Help visitors find practical guidance about planning, building and improving WordPress websites, demonstrate RWD's expertise and direct readers towards the most relevant service or audience page.
- **Audience:** Businesses planning a new website, owners of existing websites that need improvement, creative and marketing agencies needing WordPress development support, and priority industry audiences such as therapists.
- **Funnel stage:** Awareness, consideration and decision.
- **Traffic source:** Organic search, social posts, email, referrals, internal links from service and audience pages, and links from project case studies.
- **Primary offer:** Useful, experience-led guidance that helps the reader make a better website decision.
- **Primary CTA:** Explore the service or resource most relevant to the article or category being viewed.
- **Secondary CTA:** Complete the Website Momentum Scorecard or start a conversation with Mike.
- **Visitor problem:** The visitor knows they need to plan, improve or better understand a website but does not know what matters most, which option is appropriate or what to do next.
- **Desired outcome:** The visitor finds a useful route, gains confidence in RWD's judgement and moves naturally to a relevant service, audience page, project or enquiry.
- **Key objections:** "This advice will be too technical"; "This is generic SEO content"; "The article will only try to sell me something"; "My business or website is different"; "I still will not know what to prioritise."

The editorial strategy, initial topics and publishing principles are defined in [RWD Content Strategy](../../06-marketing/content-strategy.md). This document defines how that strategy should appear and behave on the website.

## Page Address

`/insights/`

Individual articles should use stable, category-independent URLs:

`/insights/[article-slug]/`

Primary category pages should use:

- `/insights/planning-a-website/`
- `/insights/improving-your-website/`
- `/insights/wordpress-explained/`
- `/insights/agency-development/`

Do not include the category in an article URL. This allows an article to be reclassified later without changing its address.

## Information Architecture

### Primary Categories

#### Planning a Website

Guidance about website briefs, scope, cost, platforms, content, choosing a partner and deciding whether to rebuild.

Primary service route: Custom WordPress Websites.

#### Improving Your Website

Guidance about auditing, prioritising and improving the performance, visibility, content, conversion and usefulness of an existing website.

Primary service route: Website Momentum.

#### WordPress Explained

Plain-English explanations of WordPress decisions that matter to businesses and teams, including Gutenberg, page builders, plugins, custom code, hosting, maintenance and technical debt.

Service route: determined by the article context.

#### Agency Development

Guidance for agencies about white-label delivery, Figma handover, design fidelity, testing, custom development, outsourcing and maintainable WordPress builds.

Primary service route: White-label WordPress Development.

### Audience Topics

Articles can also be associated with an audience such as therapists, estate agents or small businesses. Audience associations should be used to curate relevant resources on the corresponding audience landing page.

Do not automatically create indexable tag archives. Create a dedicated audience content hub only when enough distinct articles exist to make it useful in its own right.

## Category Page Template

Each category page should be an editorial landing page, not only a default WordPress archive.

Recommended structure:

1. Category heading and concise introduction
2. Recommended starting article
3. Article listing
4. Closely related service
5. Relevant project evidence where available
6. Contextual final call to action

The category introduction should explain:

- Who the guidance is for
- What problems or decisions it covers
- What the reader should be able to do after using it

Category pages should contain enough original introductory content to help a reader choose where to start. Do not add long SEO copy above the article listing merely to make the page appear substantial.

## Article Page Template

Use a consistent article structure while allowing the body to follow the subject naturally.

1. Breadcrumb
2. Primary category
3. H1 article title
4. Standfirst explaining who the article is for and what it will help them decide or do
5. Mike's byline, publication date and meaningful update date
6. Direct answer or key takeaway near the beginning
7. Main article body with descriptive headings
8. Relevant examples, screenshots or diagrams
9. Prioritised recommendations or next steps
10. Contextual service call to action
11. Relevant project or case study
12. Two or three closely related articles
13. Author information and link to My Approach

Use a table of contents only when an article is long or complex enough for it to improve navigation. Do not force every article into an identical formula or add sections that do not help answer the question.

## Calls to Action

The call to action should follow the subject of the page.

| Article context | Primary route |
| --- | --- |
| New website, rebuild, cost, scope or choosing a partner | Custom WordPress Websites |
| Audit, performance, visibility, conversion, content or ongoing improvement | Website Momentum |
| Therapist or private-practice subject | Websites for Therapists |
| Agency workflow, outsourcing or technical delivery | White-label WordPress Development |
| Technical explanation without immediate buying intent | A related article or relevant My Approach section |

Prefer one strong contextual call to action near the conclusion. A restrained mid-article link is appropriate when it directly helps the reader. Do not insert repetitive promotional panels between sections.

## Internal Linking

Every article should link to:

- The most relevant service or audience page
- At least one closely related article when one exists
- Relevant selected work when a project substantiates the subject

Service and audience pages should link back to the strongest supporting articles. Category pages should feature a deliberate starting point rather than relying entirely on chronological order.

Use descriptive link text that explains the destination. Avoid repeated generic links such as "click here" or "learn more" where a more useful description is available.

## Search and Technical Requirements

- Use one clear H1 on the Insights index, each category page and each article.
- Give every index, category and article page a distinct, descriptive SEO title and meta description.
- Use canonical URLs and stable article addresses.
- Include crawlable HTML links between articles, categories, services, audience pages and work.
- Add `BlogPosting` structured data to articles with an accurate headline, author, publication date, meaningful update date and representative image.
- Add breadcrumb structured data where implemented consistently across the site.
- Use a Person author entity for Mike and link it to a genuine author or About/My Approach page.
- Include relevant, high-quality images rather than generic technology stock photography.
- Provide descriptive alternative text when an image conveys information. Use empty alternative text for purely decorative images.
- Include article and category pages in the XML sitemap when they are intended to be indexed.
- Keep thin search results, filter combinations and tag archives out of the search index.
- Make pagination crawlable if the article library outgrows a single listing page.
- Ensure article content remains usable without relying on client-side JavaScript to render the main text or links.

## Filters and Search

Do not add site search or complex filtering at launch unless the number of articles makes browsing difficult.

The four category routes should be enough for the initial library. Audience filters can be added later if they materially help readers. Filtered combinations should not create large numbers of near-duplicate indexable URLs.

## Visual Direction

- Keep the page calm, editorial and consistent with the RWD brand.
- Use genuine screenshots, annotated interfaces, project details, diagrams and photographs of physical audit notes where they help explain the subject.
- Avoid generic laptop mock-ups, floating browser windows and decorative icons repeated across every article.
- Make category and article cards easy to scan without turning the page into a dense grid.
- Use the RWD green and amber sparingly for navigation, emphasis and progress rather than decorating every card.
- Prioritise readable line length, clear heading hierarchy and generous spacing on article pages.

## Launch Scope

The Insights section does not need every planned feature before launch.

Minimum useful launch:

- Insights index
- Individual article template
- At least four strong articles covering more than one commercial route
- Category pages only where enough related content exists to make the page useful
- Contextual service links
- Related articles
- Author and date information
- Article and breadcrumb structured data

Publish the remaining category pages as the library grows rather than launching empty or thin archives. Add the Website Momentum Scorecard, audience filters, site search and larger resource hubs when the content library and user behaviour justify them.

## Measurement

Track:

- Visits from articles to service, audience, work and contact pages
- Enquiry submissions influenced by an article
- Scorecard completions or email sign-ups when available
- Organic queries and landing pages in Google Search Console
- Engagement with recommended articles and category routes
- Social and email traffic to articles
- Articles that influence qualified pipeline or revenue

Do not judge the section solely by total page views. A lower-volume article can be valuable when it reaches a narrow, commercially relevant audience.
